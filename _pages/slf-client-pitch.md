# SLF-Grape Water Based Density Separator

## Team

LanternBye

## Clients

Cornell CALS Extension, E&J Gallo Winery, and National Grape

## Problem Statement

Grape growers are trying to collect grapes using mechanical harvesters, but both SLFs and grapes are being harvested together, contaminating the product and causing financial losses due to inefficiency. Obvious solutions are difficult to implement because SLFs do not fly off when grape vines are shaken during harvesting, causing SLFs and grapes to mix together in harvesting containers. Therefore, SLFs and grapes must be separated during the harvesting process before they are stored and crushed together.

## Impact

Over time this approach could decrease the SLF population while increasing harvest quality and yield. Ideally, it would not force growers to change their existing processing systems and equipment.

## Proposed Direction: Density Separator

**What it is:** A water-based separation system that uses density differences to separate SLFs from grapes immediately after harvesting.

**How it would be used:**

- Mechanical shaker dislodges grapes and SLFs from grapevines
- Mixture enters a controlled stream of water
- Grapes sink and are collected in a drain at the bottom
- SLFs float and are diverted to another area for collection or extermination

**Why it is better than the status quo:** There are no highly effective solutions that remove SLF from grapes before harvest, and this concept is intended to improve grape crop yield without major workflow changes.

**End-of-semester proof of concept:** We aimed to build a small-scale model that could separate a mixture of SLF and grapes with a reasonable margin of error.

## Key Risks and Unknowns

- **Strength of SLFs:** If SLFs can hold on to grapes long enough, they may still be harvested alongside them. One possible test is dropping SLFs holding grapes into water and observing their behavior. If they continue holding on, a fruit-safe soap solution could be explored.
- **Grape submersion:** Water exposure could affect grape quality or texture. Wet and dry grapes could be stored and compared over time, and drainage methods would need to be evaluated.

## Questions for the Client

1. When in processing are grapes cleaned, and could this system be integrated there to save time?
2. At what point in harvesting does pulverization occur, and when does washing significantly affect yield?
3. What grape harvester models and collection bin dimensions are most common in current use?
