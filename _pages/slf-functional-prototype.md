The completed assembly acts as a rigid body that spins as a whole to scoop up and separate SLFs from the sugar-water solution.

## Design Tests

### Motion and interference

1. **What:** Test for clashing, grinding, and interference between components.
2. **How:** Assemble the prototype, mount it into the separation container, and rotate it through a full 360 degrees.
3. **What happened:** The wheel could not fully rotate because it ground against the tapered walls of the plastic bucket.
4. **Design change:** Replace the tapered bucket with a rectangular, untapered container.

### Stability and support

1. **What:** Test whether the prototype remains stable while mounted in the container.
2. **How:** Spin the assembly using the handle and check for slipping, shifting, or catching.
3. **What happened:** The assembly slipped and fell apart easily.
4. **Design change:** Epoxy the components together and add a key to the shaft.

## Success Criteria

1. The design should be relatively quiet, indicating low friction and low energy loss.
2. It should move through the water with low effort while maintaining enough speed to collect SLF effectively.
3. It should remain intact through repeated testing and several hundred rotations.

The prototype was quiet and turned well, but bucket interference stopped motion. Friction-fit connections were also not strong enough, so tighter tolerances and epoxy were identified as necessary improvements.
