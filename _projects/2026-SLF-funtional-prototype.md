The completed assembly will be a rigid body that spins as a whole to scoop up and separate SLFs from sugar water1

Design Test:

1) What: We are going to test the motion and interference of our machined and ordered components of our water wheel (ex. clashing, grinding, etc.).

2) How: We still test if our design and machining procedures for the components of said design can work together successfully by assembling all components, mounting them to our separation container, and spinning the entire assembly with a handle to see if it functions properly within the full range expected (360º complete rotation).

a) We will fabricate the assembly by by laser cutting acrylic pieces in RPL side wheels, cutting an ordered 1” pvc pipe to size and sliding it into center circular cutouts of the side wheels, and cutting/bending a 1/16” thick piece of aluminum to size for the fins and inserting them into cut-out slots on the side wheels (and gluing all the components to make the assembly secure). 
3) What Happened: After assembling all of the components and installing these pieces into the clear container we are using for our prototype, we observed that the wheel could not fully rotate because it grinded against the walls of the plastic bucket at the bottom due to the bucket’s taper.
4) Design Changes: After observing the function of the scooping water wheel in our prototype design, we will find a plastic bucket without a taper.

1) What: We are going to test the stability and support of the prototype of our designed water wheel, which is slid in between two sides of a clear container, mounted by a 1” pvc pipe.
2) How: We still test if our mounting procedures are stable by assembling all components into the container and using the handle to spin the assembly. We will check for slide, shifting, or catching as this spinning motion occurs.  
3) What Happened: After installing this assembly into the clear container we are using for our prototype, we observed that the assembly kept slipping and fell apart easily.
4) Design Changes: After observing the function of the scooping water wheel in our prototype design, we will epoxy components together and add a key to the shaft.

Success Criteria: 
1) Design must be relatively quiet, showing a low amount of friction/ energy loss of the system.

a) The wheel was very quiet until it began to rub up against the sides of the container and then get stuck. 

2) Must be able to move against the water with low difficulty, while maintaining a speed quick enough to gather SLF without them falling out. 

a) The wheel turned very well but once again we came into contact with the bucket itself which would stop the motion. 

3) Design doesn’t fall apart (all adhesives, connections,etc. intact) over the course of testing, and can complete a few hundred rotations w/ out difficulty.

a)Trying to friction fit the pieces together did not work well. Next time we will account for more error and make sure that the sizes aren't exactly the same. We will also epoxy the pieces together for added strength and ability to withstand the water.
